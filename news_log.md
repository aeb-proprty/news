### UPDATE — COPENHAGEN HOUSING: Prices Surge to 72-76K DKK/m², Nationalbanken Sounds Alarm
Since April's report of ~62,900 DKK/m², Copenhagen apartment prices have surged to approximately 72,000-76,000 DKK/m² — up ~25% YoY. Apartments are rising roughly 100,000 DKK/month. ### CLIMATE: Why It's So Hot — Global Dimming, Record Heatwaves, and a Super El Niño Building
The Economist's July 16 piece "It's too darn hot. Blame global dimming" covers a growing body of research: decades of sulfate aerosol pollution acted as a sunshade, cooling Europe by ~1C in summer. Clean-air regulations removed this mask, revealing hidden greenhouse warming underneath. Western Europe has warmed ~2.3C since 1980 — triple the global average. A 2026 Geophysical Research Letters study confirmed warming acceleration from 0.18C to 0.31C per decade. The IMO 2020 shipping sulfur reduction contributed ~20% of 2023's record warmth. Practically: Europe's third heatwave of 2026 is ongoing. Denmark broke temperature records. France hit 44.3C (hottest since records began 1947). Over 23,580 Europeans have died from heat this summer. Meanwhile, a Super El Niño is developing — forecast to peak at ~3.6C above average, roughly 1C above any previous record. WMO confirmed El Niño conditions June 2. There is an 81% chance of the strongest El Niño ever recorded by October-December 2026, and 80%+ chance 2027 becomes the hottest year in history.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — Denmark is breaking heat records. European infrastructure and daily life are increasingly disrupted by heat events. The Super El Niño will worsen this through 2027. Energy costs rise as cooling demand increases while the Iran conflict constrains supply.
