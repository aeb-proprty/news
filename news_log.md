# Daily News Log

## 2026-05-06

### DANISH POLITICS: Coalition Talks Now Record-Breaking — Rasmussen Forces "Centre" Coalition
Six weeks after the March 24 election, Denmark's coalition talks are set to become the longest in the country's history. On April 10, Lars Løkke Rasmussen blocked any coalition involving the Red-Green Alliance, calling it "irresponsible and impossible" due to economic policy disagreements. He now demands a centre coalition of five parties from both blocs: Social Democrats, Social Liberals, Venstre, Conservatives, and his own Moderates. Frederiksen's preferred five-party red-bloc government is effectively dead. Denmark remains governed by a caretaker cabinet.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — No government means no new policy on housing, defense, or the economy. The longer this drags, the more uncertainty for Copenhagen housing regulation, tax policy, and Denmark's EU/NATO posture. The centrist coalition Rasmussen wants would likely be more market-friendly and less aggressive on housing regulation than a red-bloc government.

### HUNGARY UPDATE: Orbán Out — Magyar Wins Supermajority Landslide
On April 12, Péter Magyar's Tisza party won 141 of 199 seats (53.6% of votes) in a historic landslide, ending Viktor Orbán's 16-year rule. Turnout hit 79.6%, the highest since 1990. Magyar secured a supermajority, giving him power to amend Hungary's constitution. Orbán conceded on election night. The EU welcomed the result — Magyar is pro-EU and has pledged to unblock frozen EU funds and restore judicial independence.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM** — This reshapes EU decision-making. Hungary's veto on Ukraine aid, migration policy, and EU defense spending is effectively gone. Expect faster EU consensus on issues that were previously deadlocked by Orbán.

### MIDDLE EAST: US-Iran War Day 67 — Hormuz Reopening Turns Violent
The conflict (now Day 67) has shifted from full-scale combat to a tense quasi-ceasefire focused on the Strait of Hormuz. Iran sent a 14-point plan demanding nonaggression guarantees, sanctions relief, and a 30-day resolution timeline. The US wants a 2-month ceasefire instead. On Monday, the US military sank six Iranian boats while attempting to escort commercial ships through Hormuz. Iran attacked the UAE with missiles, and the IRGC warned a return to full war is "likely." Trump called discussions "very positive" despite the violence.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — Hormuz handles ~20% of global oil transit. The violent reopening attempt means oil prices remain volatile. Watch energy bills and fuel costs. If this escalates further, expect broader economic impacts across Europe.

### EU POLITICS: Mercosur Trade Deal Now Active — Meta Faces Massive DSA Fine
Two major EU developments: (1) The EU-Mercosur trade agreement entered provisional application on May 1, cutting tariffs on EU exports to Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, and Uruguay. 344 EU geographical indications are now protected in Mercosur countries. Over 91% of EU goods will see tariffs removed over 10 years. (2) The European Commission found Meta in breach of the Digital Services Act for failing to prevent children under 13 from using Instagram and Facebook. ~10-12% of users under 13 bypass age checks with false birthdates. Meta faces fines of up to 6% of global revenue.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM** — The Mercosur deal may marginally lower prices on South American imports (beef, coffee) and boost EU export sectors. The Meta/DSA ruling signals the EU is serious about enforcement — expect tighter age verification on all platforms operating in Europe.

### AI: Grok 4.3 Launches, Novo Nordisk Partners with OpenAI, Google Pentagon Backlash
Three significant AI developments: (1) xAI released Grok 4.3 with 1M token context, native video input, 40% price cuts, and speech APIs in 25 languages. It tops agentic benchmarks but still lacks cross-session memory. (2) Danish pharma giant Novo Nordisk announced a strategic partnership with OpenAI to integrate AI across drug discovery, clinical trials, manufacturing, and supply chains, with full deployment by end of 2026. (3) Google agreed to let Gemini AI run inside US military classified networks, prompting ~600 employees to sign an open letter opposing the deal. Separately, Anthropic released 10 preconfigured AI agents for the financial sector.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM-HIGH** — The Novo-OpenAI deal is significant for Denmark's largest company and could accelerate new obesity/diabetes treatments. Grok 4.3's pricing pressure benefits anyone using AI APIs. The Google-Pentagon deal raises questions about the ethics of AI in military applications that will drive EU AI Act enforcement.

### DENMARK INFRASTRUCTURE: Physical Rejsekort Ending May 28 — Switch to Basiskort or App
The physical Rejsekort (blue card) will be phased out starting May 28 in Jutland and Funen, with a one-month delay on Zealand. Replacement options: the Rejsekort app or the new Basiskort physical card. Basiskort Anonymous will be available at select locations by May 26. Business/commuter Basiskort launches in June. The check-in/check-out stands are being replaced with ~9,000 new card readers.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — This directly affects your daily commute. You need to transition to either the app or Basiskort before the deadline. If you're on Zealand, you have until late June, but act now to avoid disruption.

### COPENHAGEN HOUSING: Prices Plateauing After 20% Surge — Forecasters Split
Apartment prices remain at ~62,900 DKK/m² median. The 20% YoY surge from the previous update appears to be cooling. Nykredit forecasts 13.7% growth for all of 2026 (suggesting most gains already happened), while Realkredit Danmark expects more modest increases, arguing apartments have reached price levels where demand is becoming limited. Nordhavn leads at 98,000 DKK/m². Vacancy remains 1-2% citywide. Rents up ~3% YoY.

**Life Impact: VERY HIGH** — If you're actively searching (your Boligsiden alerts suggest you are), the forecaster split is important: the explosive growth phase may be ending, which could mean slightly less urgency — but supply remains structurally constrained. The political vacuum from the coalition deadlock means no new housing policy is coming anytime soon.

### 3D PRINTING: 256-Laser Metal Printer, Skin Grafts Go Clinical, UltiMaker Upgrades
Eplus3D unveiled the EP-M3050, an ultra-large-format metal powder bed fusion system with 256 lasers and a build volume of 3050×3050mm (Z-axis up to 5000mm) — industrial-scale metal AM is entering a new era. 3D printed skin grafts are moving from research to clinical use for complex wound treatment. UltiMaker announced upgrades for the S6 and S8 printers. Biochar is proving effective as a sustainable filler to improve print quality and mechanical strength in FDM parts.

**Life Impact: LOW-MEDIUM** — The UltiMaker upgrades may be relevant if you're in that ecosystem. The skin graft development is a genuinely significant medical advancement. The Eplus3D machine is industrial-only but signals where the technology is heading.

### INNOVATION: First Commercial Space Station Launching This Month
Vast Space's Haven-1 is scheduled to launch in May 2026 on a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket — the first commercial space station. The bus-sized habitat will initially support crews of four for 10-day missions and enable paying customers to conduct microgravity research. Also: humanoid robot shipments projected to grow 700% in 2026, and sodium-ion batteries (made from salt) are emerging as a cheaper, safer alternative to lithium for grid storage and affordable EVs.

**Life Impact: LOW-MEDIUM** — Haven-1 is a milestone for commercial space but won't affect daily life yet. Sodium-ion batteries could meaningfully reduce EV and energy storage costs within 2-3 years, which would affect electricity and transport costs in Denmark.
