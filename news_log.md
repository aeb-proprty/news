### DANISH POLITICS: Frederiksen Gets Second Shot After Centre-Right Talks Collapse
Update: The coalition deadlock you read about in April has only deepened — Denmark is now in its longest government formation process in modern history (63+ days and counting). Troels Lund Poulsen (Venstre) was given the formateur role on May 8 after Frederiksen's initial talks stalled, but his centre-right negotiations collapsed on May 22 over disagreements on tax cuts, infrastructure vs. welfare spending, and the budget deficit. King Frederik X reappointed Frederiksen as formateur on May 23, with support from the Moderates and the red bloc. She's now attempting to form what would be her third government.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — As a Venstre member, this directly concerns your party. Venstre's Poulsen failed to deliver a blue-bloc government, and the party is now in a weaker negotiating position. Whatever coalition emerges will shape tax policy, housing regulation, and defense spending for years. Watch what concessions Frederiksen demands from the Moderates.

### DENMARK: Nets Payment Outage Shut Down the Country for 5 Hours
On May 21, Nets — which handles ~80% of all card transactions in Denmark — suffered a complete system failure from 9 AM to 2 PM. The backup system also failed to activate. Danes couldn't pay for trains, parking, groceries, or anything card-based. Even Danes abroad were locked out of ATMs. Acting business minister Morten Bødskov demanded a formal report from the financial supervisory authority, calling it "serious" that Nets has had repeated major outages.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — This exposed Denmark's near-total dependency on a single payment provider. If you were affected (your wine email referenced it), this is a systemic risk to daily life. Expect political pressure for redundancy requirements.

### EU POLITICS: EU-US Trade Deal Clears Major Hurdle
The EU Council and Parliament reached a provisional agreement on May 20 to implement the tariff elements of the August 2025 EU-US Joint Statement. The EU will eliminate tariffs on US industrial goods; the US caps tariffs on most EU goods at 15%. There's a safeguard mechanism letting Brussels suspend reductions if US imports harm European industry, and a condition that the US must lower its steel/aluminum tariffs by end of 2026. Final vote expected mid-June, ahead of Trump's July 4 deadline.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM-HIGH** — A 15% cap on EU goods entering the US is a real win for European exporters (and Danish companies like Novo Nordisk, Vestas, LEGO). Consumer prices on US imports to Europe should drop. The safeguard clause is smart insurance.

### GEOPOLITICS: Iran-US Ceasefire Framework Reached, Strait of Hormuz Reopening Negotiated
Update: The shooting war from April has moved to fragile diplomacy. The US and Iran have developed a framework for a 60-day ceasefire extension while negotiating a "final deal." The Strait of Hormuz would be de-mined and reopened. Trump claims an agreement is "largely negotiated" but also accuses Iran of "numerous" ceasefire violations. Key sticking points: Iran refuses to send enriched uranium abroad as the US demands, and nuclear negotiations may be deferred until after the war formally ends. Pakistan's army chief is mediating.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — The Strait of Hormuz handles ~20% of global oil. Reopening it would relieve energy prices across Europe. But "largely negotiated" with Trump means nothing is done. If talks collapse, expect another oil price shock.

### 3D PRINTING: First 3D-Printed Cornea Restores Sight in Blind Patient
South Korean scientists (POSTECH/Kyungpook National University) 3D bioprinted a living human cornea using decellularized corneal stroma and stem cells, achieving >90% cell viability. For the first time ever, a 3D-printed cornea was transplanted into a legally blind patient and successfully restored their sight. Broader trial data expected H2 2026. This is a landmark moment for bioprinting.

**Life Impact: LOW-MEDIUM** — Not immediate for you, but this is genuinely historic. 12.7 million people worldwide are blind from corneal disease. If trials hold up, this changes regenerative medicine.
