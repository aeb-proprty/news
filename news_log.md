### DANISH POLITICS: Coalition Talks Collapse — Frederiksen Gets Second Chance
Two months after the March 24 snap election, Denmark still has no government. Troels Lund Poulsen (Venstre) attempted to form a centre-right coalition but failed on May 22 when the Moderates refused to back a minority government. Key disagreements: tax cuts for middle-income earners, infrastructure vs. social welfare spending, budget deficit management, climate, and immigration. After the collapse, Lars Lokke Rasmussen (Moderates) threw his support behind Mette Frederiksen, who was re-appointed as formateur by King Frederik X on May 23. She now has backing from the Moderates and red-bloc parties. This is the longest government formation process in modern Danish history.

**Life Impact: VERY HIGH** — You still have no functioning government. Tax policy, housing regulation, and green building rules (which directly affect Copenhagen property prices) are all frozen. The longer this drags, the more delayed any policy response to the housing squeeze becomes.

### GEOPOLITICS: Iran-US Deal "Largely Negotiated" — Then US Strikes Again
A fragile ceasefire has held since April 8, but on May 25-26 the US conducted "self-defense strikes" on Iranian missile launch sites and boats around the Strait of Hormuz. Trump claimed a deal is "largely negotiated" including a memorandum of understanding, with broader talks within 30-60 days. But Iran's Fars news agency says the Strait remains under Iranian management, contradicting Trump's claims. Key sticking points: nuclear program language and sanctions wording — Rubio says they're stuck on "a word, a sentence." The IRGC warned it will retaliate against ceasefire violations. Two-phase deal structure expected: Phase 1 reopens the Strait, Phase 2 covers nukes and broader issues.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — Oil prices and energy costs remain volatile. Every escalation at the Strait ripples through European fuel and electricity prices. If you drive an EV, you're somewhat insulated on fuel, but electricity prices in Denmark still correlate with gas markets.

### EU-US TRADE: Tariff Deal Clears Major Hurdle
The EU Council and Parliament reached a provisional agreement on May 20 implementing the August 2025 EU-US Joint Statement. Key terms: EU drops tariffs on US industrial goods to 0%. US caps tariffs on EU goods at 15% (down from 27.5% on autos). Includes safeguard mechanisms letting Brussels suspend reductions if US imports harm EU industry, or if the US keeps steel/aluminum tariffs above 15% by end of 2026. Final vote expected mid-June to meet Trump's July 4 deadline.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — This directly affects consumer prices in Denmark. Lower auto tariffs could bring down prices on US-made EVs. The safeguard clause matters — if it triggers, expect price volatility on imported goods.

### EU POLITICS: Parliament Calls Out Slovakia's Democratic Backsliding
On May 20, the European Parliament voted on a resolution condemning democratic backsliding under Robert Fico. The resolution states EU money and EU values are inseparable, calling for restoration of rule of law, investigation of EU funds misuse, and protection of voting rights for Slovaks abroad. Slovakia's liberal democracy score has fallen from 76.2 (2022) to 58.4 (2024). This follows the Orban pattern previously reported — the EU's internal democratic health is under sustained pressure.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM** — This shapes whether the EU can function as a cohesive bloc on defense, migration, and trade. More illiberal governments = harder consensus on issues that affect all member states.

### AI: Apple Opening iOS 27 to Claude, Gemini, and Others
Apple is preparing to let users choose their AI provider in iOS 27, iPadOS 27, and macOS 27 this fall. Internally called "Extensions," the system routes text generation, image editing, and Siri queries to providers of the user's choosing — Google (Gemini), Anthropic (Claude), and potentially others. Currently in closed testing. ChatGPT has been the only third-party slot since iOS 18. Announcement expected at WWDC in June. Users will be able to set custom Siri voices per AI provider.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM-HIGH** — If you're on Apple devices, this is a major quality-of-life upgrade. You'll get to pick which AI brain powers your phone. Given your tech interest (Humac inquiry in your inbox), worth watching WWDC.

### COPENHAGEN HOUSING: Nykredit Forecasts 13.7% Price Rise in 2026
No structural change since last update — prices remain around 62,900 DKK/m² median. But Nykredit now projects a 13.7% annual price increase for Copenhagen apartments in 2026, significantly above the 3-3.5% forecast from earlier this year. Nordhavn remains most expensive at ~98,000 DKK/m². Entry-level ownership starts around 3M DKK in outer districts like Sydhavn. Supply remains constrained by green building requirements.

**Life Impact: VERY HIGH** — You're actively searching on Boligsiden (I can see your saved searches). The revised Nykredit forecast of 13.7% means waiting is expensive — every month of delay costs roughly 1% on your purchase price. The government deadlock means no near-term housing policy relief.

### INNOVATION: First Commercial Space Station Launching This Month
Vast Space's Haven-1 is scheduled to launch in May 2026 on a SpaceX Falcon 9 — the first commercial space station. The bus-sized habitat supports crews of four for 10-day missions, enabling paid microgravity research (plant growth, drug testing). Separately, sodium-ion batteries (made from salt) are emerging as a cheaper, safer alternative to lithium for grid storage and affordable EVs, backed by major investment.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM** — Haven-1 is a milestone but won't touch your daily life yet. Sodium-ion batteries are more relevant — they could bring down EV and home battery costs within 2-3 years, which matters if you're in the EV ecosystem (Clever charging in your inbox).
