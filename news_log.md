### DANISH POLITICS: 6 Weeks Without a Government — Løkke Walks Out Again
Denmark remains without a government 6 weeks after the March 24 snap election. On Friday May 2, Lars Løkke Rasmussen (Moderaterne) left coalition talks with Mette Frederiksen after 7+ hours — exiting with a wave from his car at 9:20 PM. Løkke blocked a left-wing coalition on April 10, calling any government relying on the Red-Green Alliance "irresponsible and impossible." He's pushing for a grand centrist coalition: Social Democrats + Social Liberals + Venstre + Conservatives + Moderaterne. Venstre joined formal talks on April 11, but the math requires uncomfortable compromises from all sides. No resolution appears imminent — Løkke himself said in mid-April he doesn't expect a government "within the next month."

**Life Impact: HIGH** — Denmark is in uncharted territory for coalition deadlock. Policy paralysis affects everything from housing regulation to the Greenland defense response. If a broad centrist government forms, expect continuity on housing and defense spending; if it collapses toward new elections, all bets are off.

### IRAN-US WAR: Ceasefire Near Collapse — Day 66, Iran Hits UAE
Major escalation since the last update. A Pakistan-mediated ceasefire was agreed April 8 and Trump extended it indefinitely on April 21. But it's now unraveling: on May 4 (Day 66), Iranian missiles were intercepted over Dubai for the first time since the truce. The US sank 6 Iranian small boats in the Strait of Hormuz. Trump announced "Project Freedom" to escort ships through the strait. Iran submitted a 14-point counter-proposal on May 2, but no negotiated settlement appears close. Trump told Congress the ceasefire "terminated" the conflict to satisfy war powers deadlines — a legal fiction given active hostilities.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — Oil prices remain elevated. If the ceasefire formally collapses, expect immediate fuel price spikes in Denmark/EU. The Strait of Hormuz handles ~20% of global oil transit. EU energy policy is already strained.

### EU: Meta Charged Under Digital Services Act — Could Face $8B+ Fine
The European Commission on April 29 formally charged Meta with failing to keep children under 13 off Instagram and Facebook. Findings: age verification is basically non-existent (kids just enter a false birthdate), and reporting an underage account requires up to 7 clicks. Roughly 10-12% of European children under 13 are active on these platforms. If confirmed, the fine could be up to 6% of Meta's global turnover — exceeding $8 billion. Total EU Big Tech fines have now topped €7 billion in 2 years, with the Trump administration expressing increasing anger over the enforcement pattern.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM** — Signals aggressive EU tech regulation continuing. If you use Meta platforms for business (proprty.ai marketing?), platform changes to comply may affect reach/targeting. Broader signal: DSA has real teeth.

### EU: Mercosur Trade Deal Goes Live
On May 1, the EU-Mercosur trade agreement started provisional application — cutting tariffs on EU exports (cars, pharma, agri-food) to Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, and Uruguay. This is one of the largest trade deals ever, covering ~780 million people.

**Life Impact: LOW-MEDIUM** — Indirect effect through cheaper imports and new export opportunities. More relevant if you hold investments in EU automotive/pharma. Danish agricultural exporters may benefit.

### AI: Cerebras IPO May 14 — Oversubscribed 3x, Valued at $27B
AI chipmaker Cerebras (maker of the world's largest chip, key inference partner to OpenAI) will IPO on Nasdaq as "CBRS" on May 14. Target price $115-$125/share for a ~$26.6B valuation. Banks already have $10B in orders for $3.5B of shares — massively oversubscribed. This is the biggest pure-play AI infrastructure IPO since NVIDIA's rise.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM** — Signals the AI hardware boom is not slowing. If you're building AI products (proprty.ai), more competition in AI chips = better inference pricing medium-term. Also a barometer for AI market sentiment.

### AI: Google Gives Pentagon Full Gemini Access — 600 Employees Protest
Google signed a deal allowing its Gemini AI models inside US military classified networks for "any lawful purpose." Nearly 600 employees signed an open letter opposing it. OpenAI and xAI have similar military deals. This marks the definitive end of the "don't be evil" era and the normalization of military AI across all major providers.

**Life Impact: LOW-MEDIUM** — No direct personal impact, but signals the direction of AI governance. Military AI contracts accelerate capability development that eventually flows to commercial products.

### COPENHAGEN HOUSING: Nykredit Forecasts 13.7% Price Rise in 2026
Updated forecasts are more bullish than the last report. Nykredit now expects Copenhagen apartment prices to rise 13.7% in 2026 (the highest estimate among major analysis houses), followed by 3.1% in 2027. Current median remains ~62,900 DKK/m². Vacancy for rentals: 1-2%. The market remains firmly a seller's market with shrinking inventory.

**Life Impact: VERY HIGH** — If you own in Copenhagen (which you do — E/F Fionia Hus II), your equity is growing significantly. If you're looking to buy more, the window continues to tighten. The 13.7% forecast is aggressive but reflects real supply constraints.

### 3D PRINTING: Eplus3D Unveils 256-Laser Metal Printer (3050mm Build Plate)
Eplus3D announced the EP-M3050 — a metal powder bed fusion system with 256 lasers and a 3050×3050mm build area (Z-axis up to 5000mm). This is industrial/aerospace scale, enabling one-piece manufacturing of structures previously impossible to print. In industry news: Xometry stock up 14% on earnings optimism; biochar emerging as a sustainable FFF filler material improving part strength while reducing porosity.

**Life Impact: LOW** — The Eplus3D machine is industrial-only (likely $1M+), but signals where metal AM is heading. Biochar filament research could eventually yield interesting consumer materials. No immediate action needed.
