## 2026-06-08

### DENMARK: New Four-Party Coalition Formed After Record 69-Day Deadlock
*Continuing story — previously covered coalition negotiations on Apr 7.*

The talks are over. On June 2, Mette Frederiksen announced a centre-left minority "four-leaf clover" coalition: Social Democrats, Moderates (Lars Løkke Rasmussen), Green Left/SF, and Social Liberals (Radikale Venstre). They hold only 82 of 179 seats — governing as a minority dependent on ad-hoc support from the Red-Green Alliance and Alternative.

**Key policy commitments:**
- **Cost of living:** VAT on all food halved (25% → ~12.5%); VAT on fruit/vegetables abolished entirely. Free dental care. Free public transport for under-22s.
- **Tax reform:** Abolition of "middle tax" and "top-top tax"; new simplified two-tier income tax. Business tax cut of 3 percentage points over three years.
- **Climate:** 85% emissions reduction target by 2035; conversion of 390,000 hectares to nature/forest; CO2 tax on livestock.
- **Immigration:** New deportation legislation; continued pursuit of asylum processing outside the EU.
- **Social media ban** for children under 15.
- **Great Prayer Day restored** as public holiday from 2030 (conditional on employment growth).

Lars Løkke Rasmussen retained as Foreign Minister — a deliberate signal of continuity on the Greenland crisis with the US. Denmark's defense spending target is now **5% of GDP by 2030** (up from 3% in 2025), with a 50 billion DKK acceleration fund. This is driven by both the Russian threat and the need to demonstrate Denmark can defend its own Arctic territory.

**Life Impact: VERY HIGH** — The VAT cuts on food will directly lower your grocery bill. The tax reform affects take-home pay. Defense spending at 5% GDP will create capacity pressures in the economy. The coalition's housing and climate policies will shape Copenhagen's development trajectory.

### MIDDLE EAST: Iran War Hits Day 100 — Ceasefire Collapsing, Hormuz Still Closed
*Continuing story — previously covered at Day 38 on Apr 7.*

The conflict has escalated far beyond the April situation. On June 8, Israel and Iran traded retaliatory missile strikes — the most serious exchange since an April 8 ceasefire was reached. The Strait of Hormuz remains largely closed. The IEA calls this the "largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market" — 20% of global oil trade normally passes through the strait.

**Brent crude has surged to ~$106/barrel** (up from $69 in 2025). Pakistan is attempting mediation, with Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi delivering a "special letter" to Iran's new Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei on June 6-7. The core impasse: Washington demands IRGC nuclear disarmament; Tehran refuses to surrender its enriched uranium stockpile.

**Life Impact: VERY HIGH** — This is the story driving everything else. Energy prices affect your heating, transport, and grocery costs. It's pushing ECB rate hikes (see housing section), driving inflation, and disrupting European LNG supply. Watch your energy bills.

### EU: Turnberry Trade Deal Heads to Parliament Vote June 16
The EU-US "Turnberry accord" faces a full Parliament plenary vote on June 16 in Strasbourg. Under the deal, the EU removes duties on most US industrial goods; the US caps tariffs on most EU exports (including cars) at 15%, down from Trump's threatened 25%. The Trade Committee approved it 31-6-3 on June 2.

Key safeguards: a "snap-back" provision if the US doesn't lift steel/aluminium tariffs by end of 2026, and a sunset clause ending the deal December 31, 2029. The US set a July 4 deadline — after which auto tariffs escalate to 25%.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — If this fails, 25% tariffs on European cars would hit Danish exports and the broader EU economy. If it passes, it stabilizes trade but critics say it rewards coercive tariff threats.

### COPENHAGEN HOUSING: Prices Hit 75,183 DKK/m² — ECB Rate Hike Imminent
*Continuing story — previously covered at 62,900 DKK/m² on Apr 7.*

Prices have accelerated well beyond the April baseline. An average 80m² Copenhagen apartment now costs ~6.0 million DKK — up 1.2 million DKK in a single year (+25% YoY). Nordhavn leads at ~98,000 DKK/m², Carlsberg Byen at 89,000 DKK/m², Frederiksberg at 77,000 DKK/m².

**Critical date: June 11 (in 3 days)** — markets price a 99% probability of a 25 bps ECB rate hike to 2.25%. Average mortgage rates already rose to 3.11%. There's debate about whether Nationalbanken will need an independent rate hike beyond the ECB due to krone weakness (trading at 7.47 DKK/EUR).

**New construction:** Enghave Brygge Syd delivering 1,000 new homes including 320 social housing units (largest since the 1980s). But the pipeline (~160,000 m²/year) falls well short of the ~2,000 units/year needed to match projected population growth of 113,000 by 2060.

**Forecasts:** Nykredit expects Copenhagen apartments +13.7% in 2026, then +3.1% in 2027. The new coalition's tax cuts (~4,000 DKK annual savings) are boosting disposable income and further fueling demand.

**Risk to watch:** Potential rent cap legislation on post-1991 apartments could affect investor appetite and rental supply.

**Life Impact: VERY HIGH** — If you're considering buying, the ECB decision on Wednesday could shift mortgage costs. Prices are up 25% YoY with structural undersupply continuing. The new coalition's food VAT cuts free up household budget but tax cuts also fuel more housing demand.
