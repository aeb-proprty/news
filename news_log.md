### DANISH POLITICS: Frederiksen Out — Troels Lund Poulsen Has Two Weeks to Form Government
**Update from Apr 7:** The coalition deadlock you read about has broken — but not in Frederiksen's favor. After a record-breaking 6+ weeks of failed negotiations, her bid for a third term collapsed on May 8. King Frederik asked Venstre leader Troels Lund Poulsen to try instead. He began meeting all parties Monday/Tuesday and has a hard two-week deadline. Every possible coalition is contradictory: Dansk Folkeparti will only back a centre-right government on strict anti-immigration conditions and refuses to sit with Social Democrats or the Moderates. The red bloc still holds 84 seats vs. blue's 77, with the Moderates' 14 seats as kingmakers. Frederiksen could still emerge — but the momentum has decisively shifted right.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — This directly shapes Denmark's next government. A Venstre-led coalition would likely mean different tax policy, housing regulation, and immigration stance than a Frederiksen continuation. If you're in Copenhagen and on a condo board, housing policy direction matters.

### MIDDLE EAST: Iran War Day 74 — Ceasefire on "Life Support," Oil Above $100
**Update from Apr 7:** The conflict has now stretched to 74 days. The ceasefire you read about (agreed April 7-8) held briefly but is now crumbling. Trump called it on "massive life support" on May 11. Iran submitted a counterproposal demanding war reparations, full Hormuz sovereignty, sanctions removal, and unfreezing of assets — Trump rejected it as "totally unacceptable." Iran vowed to "never bow." Israel killed 24 in Lebanon on May 9. Oil is now above $100/barrel and the US DOE warns it will stay there. Gas prices heading toward $5/gallon in the US. Thousands dead, millions displaced across Iran, Lebanon, Israel, and Gulf states.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — Energy costs are hitting consumers globally. Even in Denmark, elevated oil prices flow through to heating, transport, and goods prices. The Strait of Hormuz disruption is structural until a deal is reached.

### EU POLITICS: Social Media Ban for Children Coming This Summer
Von der Leyen announced Monday that the EU will propose legislation banning social media for minors, potentially as early as this summer. A "Special Panel of experts on Child Safety Online" is reporting back by July. The Commission has built its own age-verification app with what it calls "the highest privacy standards in the world." Brussels has already placed Instagram and Snapchat under scrutiny under the Digital Services Act. France will be the first EU country to ban social media for under-15s (September). A YouGov poll shows majority support across six EU countries for banning under-16s.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM** — If you have or plan to have kids, this will reshape how they use the internet. For developers, it means new compliance requirements around age verification. The Digital Fairness Act may also ban "addictive design" features in apps.

### COPENHAGEN HOUSING: Market Steady — Nykredit Forecasts 13.7% Growth in 2026
No dramatic shift since April, but Nykredit has published the most bullish forecast among major analysts: 13.7% apartment price growth in Copenhagen for 2026, slowing to 3.1% in 2027. Current median remains ~62,900 DKK/m². Most sales close within 3% of asking price, and well-located properties sell in 14-21 days. Nordhavn is the priciest neighborhood at 98,000 DKK/m² — 30,000 DKK/m² more than Valby. Your Boligsiden alert showed 4 new listings and 8 price changes yesterday in your saved "hot-swap" search.

**Life Impact: VERY HIGH** — If you're actively searching (and your Boligsiden alerts confirm you are), the Nykredit forecast suggests buying sooner rather than later. The spread between neighborhoods is widening.

### 3D PRINTING: Japan Builds First 3D-Printed Two-Story House
Startup Kizuki and 20+ partner companies unveiled "Stealth House" — Japan's first fully 3D-printed two-story residential home, marking the first time design-to-construction was completed at two-story scale. In other news: Modix announced the MAMA-1000 (1m³ build volume, interchangeable filament/pellet heads), and Durham University received £125K to develop 3D-printed custom surgical plates for facial reconstruction. Rolls-Royce opened a new metal AM facility in Bristol for aircraft engine components.

**Life Impact: LOW-MEDIUM** — The Japan house story signals 3D-printed construction reaching practical scale. The Modix MAMA-1000 is interesting if you're considering large-format printing — pellet heads offer much lower material costs.
