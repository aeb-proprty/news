### US-IRAN: Ceasefire Collapses, Strait of Hormuz Blocked Again
The June 17 Doha ceasefire between the US and Iran collapsed on July 8-9. The US struck 90 targets along Iran's coastline; Iran retaliated with ballistic missiles at US bases in Kuwait, Bahrain, and Jordan, and again blocked the Strait of Hormuz. Trump declared the ceasefire "over" at the NATO Ankara summit. However, by July 10-11 the tone shifted — Qatari mediators traveled to Tehran on July 11 to restart talks, and US officials confirmed "technical talks for a lasting peace deal will continue." No large vessels have crossed the strait since July 8. Brent crude sits at $76.58/barrel — surprisingly contained due to alternative supply routes and strategic reserve releases. Khamenei's state funeral (killed Feb 28) concluded July 9; Iran's new Supreme Leader's whereabouts were initially kept secret during mourning.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — Hormuz closure directly affects global energy prices. If you drive, heat with gas, or buy anything shipped internationally, this matters. The relatively contained oil price ($76.58) suggests markets have adapted, but renewed escalation could spike costs quickly. Denmark's extended border controls with Germany (starting today, July 12) are partly a response to the broader security environment.

### COPENHAGEN HOUSING: Nationalbank Warns of Expectation-Driven Price Spiral
Danmarks Nationalbank published an analysis warning that Copenhagen apartment prices have risen ~14-20% YoY, with signs purchases are increasingly driven by expectations of further gains rather than fundamentals. Median prices sit around DKK 75,000/sqm (a standard 80sqm apartment ~DKK 6M / EUR 803K). Listings fell below 1,000 owner-occupied apartments at one point — a historic low. The central bank urged maintaining sound lending standards, warning that expectation-driven demand could amplify consequences of any economic downturn. Supply remains constrained: new construction permits face 8-12% higher costs from environmental requirements, reducing annual new housing supply by ~15%.

**Life Impact: VERY HIGH** — You own in Fionia Hus II, so your equity is growing significantly. But the Nationalbank's warning is notable — it's the kind of language central banks use before tightening lending rules. If you're considering buying/selling/refinancing, the window of easy credit may narrow. The structural supply shortage (green building regs) isn't going away.

### DANISH POLITICS: Property Valuation Bill Fast-Tracked Before Summer Recess
The government introduced a bill (June 16) to delay new property valuations yet again, replacing 2026-2027 assessments with a simpler system based on 2022-2023 valuations adjusted for local price changes. Tax Minister Jakob Engel-Schmidt said it reduces the risk of unexpected tax bills for homeowners. Fast-tracked for passage before the July summer recess. Separately, Denmark's new minority coalition (Social Democrats + SF + Radikale Venstre + Moderates, formed June 1 after 69 days of negotiations — the longest in Danish history) holds 82 of 179 seats.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — This directly affects your property taxes. The delayed valuation means your tax won't suddenly jump to reflect the 14-20% price surge this year. Good news for homeowners short-term, but kicks the can down the road — eventual reassessment will hit harder.

### AI: Apple Sues OpenAI for Trade Secret Theft
Apple filed a federal lawsuit on July 10 accusing OpenAI of systematically stealing trade secrets related to AI hardware. Apple names OpenAI's Chief Hardware Officer Tang Tan (former Apple VP) as a defendant, alleging he directed Apple employees to share secrets during hiring interviews. A second defendant, former Apple employee Chang Liu, is accused of stealing an Apple laptop. Apple claims OpenAI hired 400+ former Apple employees as part of the scheme. Apple seeks an injunction and damages — a dramatic reversal given their 2024 Siri/ChatGPT partnership.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM-HIGH** — This signals the AI hardware war is escalating. If OpenAI's hardware ambitions get injuncted, it could slow their model deployment and shift competitive dynamics. For anyone building on OpenAI's API, watch for disruption risk.

### EU: Commission Finds Meta in Breach of DSA Over Addictive Design
On July 10, the European Commission issued a preliminary finding that Instagram and Facebook breach the Digital Services Act through addictive design features (infinite scroll, autoplay, push notifications, hyper-personalized algorithms) harming minors and vulnerable adults. Meta faces potential fines of up to 6% of global annual turnover (~$12 billion). Meta disputes the findings.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM** — Sets precedent for how EU regulates attention-economy design patterns. If you build digital products (Byens Profiler?), the DSA's stance on "addictive design" will likely trickle down to smaller platforms eventually.

### NATO ANKARA SUMMIT: Defense Spending Target Raised to 5% GDP by 2035
NATO allies pledged EUR 70 billion ($80B) in Ukraine aid for 2026 and 2027. Defense spending target raised to 5% of GDP by 2035 (up from the old 2% target). European allies increased core defense investment by $139B since The Hague 2025 summit. Denmark, as a NATO member, will need to dramatically increase military spending — currently around 2.4% of GDP.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM-HIGH** — Denmark going from 2.4% to 5% GDP defense spending means either higher taxes, spending cuts elsewhere, or both. This is a multi-year fiscal restructuring that will affect public budgets significantly.

### DENMARK: Bars Ukrainian Men of Conscription Age from New Residence Permits
Effective June 25, Denmark stopped granting new residence permits to Ukrainian men aged 23-60. Immigration Minister Morten Bodskov said it prevents using Danish protection to evade mobilization. The 47,600 Ukrainians already in Denmark are unaffected. Denmark is the first EU country to implement such a restriction (enabled by its opt-out from EU justice/home affairs rules).

**Life Impact: LOW** — Unless you employ or know Ukrainian men in this age bracket seeking permits. Signals the government's stance on immigration policy under the new coalition.

### EU: Air Passenger Rights Overhaul Approved After 13-Year Deadlock
Key changes: 250-600 EUR compensation for delays over 3 hours maintained; free carry-on personal item guaranteed on all flights; families with children under 14 sit together without extra charge; airlines cannot void return flights if outbound is skipped; airlines must explain compensation decisions within 30 days. Awaits formal Council confirmation by early August.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM** — You use Rejsekort daily and likely fly within Europe. The "skip leg" protection and guaranteed free carry-on are genuinely useful consumer wins.

### INNOVATION: China Recovers Rocket Booster on Maiden Flight
China's Long March 10B launched July 10 and recovered its first stage using a sea-based net-capture system — the first time anyone has achieved booster recovery on an inaugural flight. China becomes only the second country (after SpaceX) to recover an orbital-class booster. Plans to refly the booster before year-end.

**Life Impact: LOW** — Signals accelerating space competition and commercial launch cost reduction. Long-term implications for satellite internet, space manufacturing.

### EU: "Mini Chat Control" Extended to 2028 in Controversial Vote
European Parliament voted (331 vs 304) to extend rules allowing platforms to voluntarily scan private messages for CSAM until April 2028. Procedurally controversial — a motion to reject got a majority of voting MEPs (314 vs 276) but failed to reach the required absolute majority of 361. Greens MEP accused Parliament of violating its own rules.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM** — End-to-end encryption protections remain under pressure in the EU. If you use Signal/WhatsApp for sensitive communications, this debate isn't over.
