### US-IRAN: Ceasefire Collapses, Strait of Hormuz Near-Shutdown
*Continuing from Apr 7 — major escalation since then.* After Pakistan brokered a ceasefire (April 8) and Trump/Pezeshkian signed the Islamabad MoU (June 17) for a 60-day negotiation period, things fell apart this week. Iran attacked three commercial vessels July 6-7, and Trump declared the ceasefire "over." The US hit 80+ Iranian targets July 7, Iran struck back at US bases in Kuwait, Bahrain, Jordan, and Qatar on July 8, and by July 9-10 the US had hit 300+ targets total. On July 11, Iran's IRGC attacked the container ship GFS Galaxy (1 crew missing) and declared the strait "closed until further notice." Strait traffic plunged from ~130 daily transits pre-war to just 5 on July 9-10. The US hit 140 more targets. Oman is proposing a dual-corridor solution (Omani waters for free navigation, Iranian waters requiring approval). Trump tempered rhetoric on July 13, saying it "would not lead to long-term military action." Oil at $76-80/barrel (down from $126 peak in March), shipping insurance up from 0.25% to 8% of vessel value, ~6,000 seafarers stranded.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — Shipping surcharges of $1,500-2,000/container are feeding through to consumer goods prices in Europe. Energy prices have stabilized somewhat but remain volatile. Denmark's new government faces pressure on defense spending as NATO just set a 5% GDP target by 2035.

### EU POLITICS: Chat Control Passes Despite Majority Opposition, Air Passenger Rights Upgraded
The EU Parliament plenary (July 7-10) had several major votes. Most controversially, "Chat Control" passed on July 9 in a procedural quirk: 314 MEPs voted against vs 276 for, but since it was a second reading, opponents needed an absolute majority of 361 to block it — they fell 47 short. The regulation allows platforms to voluntarily scan for CSAM until April 2028, but Parliament added an amendment exempting end-to-end encrypted services (returning to Council). New air passenger rights passed 646-12: compensation of €250-600 for 3+ hour delays, mandatory free child seating adjacent to adults for under-14s, free personal cabin item, and 30-day airline response deadline. The EU-Mexico trade deal was approved (479-119). Anti-dumping duties of 4.3-45.3% on Chinese tires took effect July 8. The EU Migration Pact took effect June 12, requiring all member states to contribute to a solidarity pool of 21,000 relocations or €420M. NATO summit set defense spending target at 5% GDP by 2035 — only 5 of 32 members currently meet even 3.5%.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM-HIGH** — Chat Control is significant for digital privacy, though the encryption exemption limits immediate impact. The air passenger rights upgrade is directly useful for frequent flyers. Chinese tire tariffs may affect car tire prices. Defense spending targets will put pressure on Danish government budgets.

### COPENHAGEN HOUSING: First Signs of Cooling — Frederiksberg Dips, Supply Surges 25%
*Continuing from Apr 7 — significant shift in market dynamics.* Copenhagen apartments now average ~76,098 DKK/m² (up from ~66,226 in April), and prices are up 26.7% YoY. But the market may be at an inflection point. TV2 reported July 11 that Frederiksberg (83,000 DKK/m², Denmark's most expensive) posted its first monthly price decline (-0.1%) in years. More notably, Copenhagen apartment supply jumped 25% YoY to 1,630 units — the opposite of the national trend (down 13.5%). Brokers are increasingly reducing listing prices. Danmarks Nationalbank issued two warnings (May 26, June 10) that price growth is "increasingly expectation-driven" with speculative purchasing behavior emerging, and urged that lending rules must not be relaxed. The ECB hiked rates 25bps on June 11 (first increase in 3 years, driven by Iran-war energy inflation) — DKK is pegged to EUR so this directly affects Danish mortgage rates, which rose to 3.11% in March. Variable-rate loans (72% of new mortgages) jumped 0.35 percentage points. Nykredit expects Copenhagen apartment growth to slow significantly in H2, moderating to 2-3% in 2027-2028. The construction pipeline (160,000 m²/year for 2025-2027) is less than half the 2020-2024 average. The new left-wing city government (mayor Sisse Marie Welling, Green Left) faces pressure on affordability — five parties support reintroducing rent ceilings on post-1991 private rentals, affecting 200,000+ units.

**Life Impact: VERY HIGH** — If you own in Copenhagen (like your E/F Fionia hus II), your equity has grown enormously but the central bank is flagging risk. The first Frederiksberg dip and supply surge are early signals of normalization. Rising mortgage rates (ECB hike + variable rate increases) will squeeze affordability further. If you're thinking of buying, the pace is finally decelerating — but structural supply shortage means no crash is expected.
