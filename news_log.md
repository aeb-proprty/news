### IRAN WAR: Ceasefire Holding But Strait of Hormuz Still Strangled — Oil at $108

Since last update (Day 38): The US and Iran agreed to a ceasefire on April 7-8, which Trump has since extended indefinitely. However, the Strait of Hormuz remains far below pre-war capacity. Iran's foreign minister said the Strait is "open" during the ceasefire but warned it will "never return to its previous state." Iran offered to fully reopen shipping if the US drops its naval blockade — but with zero nuclear concessions, which Trump insists on. Brent crude hit $108/barrel yesterday, up 55% since the war began. Putin met Iran's foreign minister in St. Petersburg; Tehran is considering US-mediated talks via Pakistan. Today, Trump's team is reviewing an Iranian peace plan, while the UN is calling for Hormuz to reopen.

**Life Impact: VERY HIGH** — Oil at $108 means energy and transport costs remain elevated across Europe. If talks collapse, $120+ oil is back on the table. Every trip to the pump and every heating bill reflects this.

### DANISH POLITICS: Coalition Talks Stalled — Løkke Blocks Red-Green, Venstre Hemorrhaging Voters

Frederiksen's coalition talks hit a wall on April 10 when Moderates leader Løkke Rasmussen vetoed any government reliant on the Red-Green Alliance, calling it "irresponsible." Frederiksen pivoted to engage Venstre and Conservatives, but Venstre's Troels Lund Poulsen refused to govern with the Social Democrats and counter-proposed a centre-right government — which Løkke rejected. The Moderates rejoined talks on April 22, but experts now expect no government until May at the earliest. Meanwhile, the Venstre newsletter you received yesterday is sobering: the party lost 40% of its voters in the Copenhagen area since 2022, and nearly 60% in Københavns Storkreds. The regional board is forming a working group to present a recovery plan before summer. Separately, Region Østdanmark is facing a proposed 100M DKK cut to public transit — Venstre is pushing back, arguing the cuts stem from Christiansborg's 2024 health reform funding reallocation, not regional mismanagement.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — No government means no new policy on housing, defense, or taxes. The Venstre voter collapse in Copenhagen signals a real party crisis. The transit cuts could directly affect commuters in the greater Copenhagen area.

### EU POLITICS: Parliament Votes on €1.78 Trillion Budget Tomorrow — Middle East Debate Today

The European Parliament plenary in Strasbourg this week is dominated by the next EU long-term budget (2028-2034). The Budget Committee proposes €1.78 trillion (constant 2025 prices), a 10% increase over the Commission's proposal, with the increase split across national plans, competitiveness/Horizon funds, and Global Europe. The vote is tomorrow (April 29). Today, MEPs debate the Middle East crisis and its impact on energy prices. Also on the agenda: amendments to ETS2 (emissions trading for buildings and road transport), a common EU methodology for transport emissions calculation, and new EU rules to protect dogs and cats from abusive breeding practices.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — The long-term budget determines EU research funding, green transition money, and defense spending for the next 7 years. ETS2 amendments will directly affect heating and transport costs in Denmark.

### AI: DeepSeek V4 Drops — Open-Source, 1M Context, Fraction of the Price

DeepSeek released V4 on April 24 in two variants: V4-Pro (1.6T params, 49B active) and V4-Flash (284B params, 13B active), both with 1 million token context. V4-Pro beats all open models on math and coding, trailing only Gemini 3.1-Pro (closed) on world knowledge. The efficiency gains are dramatic: V4-Pro needs only 27% of inference FLOPs and 10% of KV cache vs. V3.2. Pricing is aggressive — Flash at $0.14/M input tokens, Pro at $1.74/M. Fully open-source as usual. Separately, Cambridge researchers published a brain-inspired chip using modified hafnium oxide that could cut AI hardware energy use by 70%. The device achieves switching currents a million times lower than conventional memristors and hundreds of stable conductance levels. Main catch: manufacturing requires 700°C, higher than standard fab processes.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM-HIGH** — DeepSeek V4 is immediately usable and dramatically cheaper than Western closed models. If you self-host AI or build with APIs, this shifts the cost calculus. The Cambridge chip is longer-term but signals where hardware is heading.

### YOUR MAIL: Father's Property Valuation Complaints — What You Should Know

Your father emailed about filing complaints (klage) over property valuations on two addresses in Greve. Key context: Denmark's new property valuation system has been plagued by delays — the Assessment Agency is still catching up with 2020 valuations. The current 2024 indexed valuations (basis for 2025-2026 taxes) are calculated automatically and generally cannot be appealed. For final valuations, you have 90 days to file a complaint (costs 1,300 DKK, refunded if successful). The complaint authority follows a "20% rule" — valuations have been upheld even when 19.5% too high, but overturned at 20.3% deviation. Filing is done through the Tax Appeal Authority's portal (Skatteankestyrelsen).

**Life Impact: MEDIUM** — Directly relevant to your family. The 20% threshold and 90-day window are the key numbers to know.
