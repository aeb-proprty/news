# Daily News Log

## 2026-05-20

### DANISH POLITICS: Troels Lund Poulsen Announces VLAK Government Framework — Frederiksen Era Ending
UPDATE from April 7: Coalition talks have broken record duration. Mette Frederiksen's attempts to form a centre-left government collapsed on May 8. The King appointed Troels Lund Poulsen (Venstre) as royal investigator. On May 19, Lund Poulsen announced he has reached a political framework with Liberal Alliance and the Conservatives for a VLAK minority government (46 seats: V-18, LA-15, K-13) with himself as PM. The package targets 60 billion DKK in prosperity growth by 2035, includes a national pesticide ban, defence strengthening, a new citizenship agreement, and fuel price measures. Dansk Folkeparti says it won't bring down the government. The critical question: Lars Løkke Rasmussen (Moderaterne) wants his party IN government with ministers and wants a centrist government — neither demand is met. If Løkke withholds support, the maths get very tight.

**Life Impact: VERY HIGH** — Denmark is likely days away from a new right-of-centre government. This means new tax policy, immigration rules, housing regulation, and defence spending priorities. The pesticide ban and fuel price measures are concrete bread-and-butter policy. As a Copenhagen homeowner, watch whether housing deregulation appears in the final deal.

### COPENHAGEN HOUSING: Prices Surge Past 83,000 DKK/m² — But Supply Finally Growing
UPDATE from April 7: Prices have accelerated beyond the earlier 62,900 DKK/m² median. March 2026 data shows Copenhagen apartment prices hit 83,111 DKK/m² — a 17.4% annual increase. However, supply is finally loosening: 1,395 apartments for sale in Copenhagen Municipality at the start of May, up 13% month-over-month and 41% since January. Nykredit forecasts 13.7% total price growth for 2026 (most already baked in) then 3.1% in 2027. Still a seller's market, but the supply increase is the first structural shift in months.

**Life Impact: VERY HIGH** — You're actively searching on Boligsiden. The supply increase is the first real sign of breathing room, but prices are still climbing. The incoming VLAK government's housing policy stance could change the dynamics — worth watching the final coalition agreement closely.

### US-IRAN: Ceasefire on "Massive Life Support" — Pakistan Mediating
UPDATE from April 7: The conflict has evolved from active escalation to fragile, unstable ceasefire. A monthlong truce is in place but Trump called it on "massive life support" on May 11. Iran submitted a new response to a US proposal via mediator Pakistan on May 18. Trump postponed a "scheduled attack" after requests from Qatar and Saudi Arabia. Core deadlock: Iran wants sanctions lifted and frozen assets released first, deferring nuclear talks; US wants immediate nuclear concessions. Iran proposes a phased approach — end war first, lift sanctions, then discuss nuclear program later.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — Oil price volatility continues to affect energy costs and inflation in Denmark/EU. A collapse of the ceasefire would spike fuel prices further — relevant given the VLAK government's fuel price measures.

### EU POLITICS: Parliament Passes Major Foreign Investment Screening Rules
The European Parliament approved (May 19) a significantly tougher FDI screening framework. All EU member states will now be required to screen foreign investments in defence, semiconductors, AI, critical raw materials, energy, and financial services. AI screening specifically targets general-purpose AI models suitable for space/defence applications or models with systemic risks under the EU AI Act. Separately, MEPs are quizzing Kaja Kallas on Middle East policy, and the Commission proposed €144M in solidarity funding for Spain, Romania, and Cyprus for 2025 climate disasters.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM-HIGH** — The mandatory FDI screening across all 27 member states is a major shift in EU economic sovereignty policy. If you work with or invest in AI/tech, the new screening rules will affect cross-border deals and partnerships. Denmark will need to align its screening mechanisms.

### 3D PRINTING: Eplus3D Breaks 3-Meter Barrier, Rolls-Royce Opens AM Cell
Eplus3D announced the EP-M3050, an ultra-large-format metal powder bed fusion system with 256 lasers and a build volume of 3050×3050mm (Z-axis up to 5000mm) — the first to break the 3-meter barrier in metal AM. Rolls-Royce opened a dedicated AM Development Cell in Bristol for fabricating complex aircraft engine components. MODIX launched a new large-format FDM printer. On the research side, Durham University received £125K for 3D-printed custom surgical plates for facial reconstruction, and biodegradable zinc implants are showing promise. Biochar is emerging as a sustainable filler that improves print quality while reducing porosity.

**Life Impact: LOW-MEDIUM** — The Eplus3D machine is industrial-scale, but signals that metal AM is becoming viable for ever-larger parts. The biochar research is interesting for sustainability-minded makers. No major consumer/desktop news this cycle.

## 2026-04-07

### DANISH POLITICS: Coalition Talks Drag On After Historic Election
The March 24 snap election left no bloc with a majority. Mette Frederiksen's Social Democrats won 38 seats but with their worst vote share (21.9%) since 1903. The Green Left surged to become the second-largest party (20 seats), and the Danish People's Party made significant gains. The Moderates are now kingmaker. Coalition negotiations are ongoing with no resolution yet. The Greenland/Trump crisis was the catalyst for the snap election and gave Frederiksen a rally-round-the-flag boost.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — A new government will directly affect tax policy, housing regulation, immigration rules, and Denmark's defense posture. If you're in Copenhagen, coalition outcomes on housing and green policy matter immediately.
