### DANISH POLITICS: Coalition Talks Drag On After Historic Election
The March 24 snap election left no bloc with a majority. Mette Frederiksen's Social Democrats won 38 seats but with their worst vote share (21.9%) since 1903. The Green Left surged to become the second-largest party (20 seats), and the Danish People's Party made significant gains. The Moderates are now kingmaker. Coalition negotiations are ongoing with no resolution yet. The Greenland/Trump crisis was the catalyst for the snap election and gave Frederiksen a rally-round-the-flag boost.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — A new government will directly affect tax policy, housing regulation, immigration rules, and Denmark's defense posture. If you're in Copenhagen, coalition outcomes on housing and green policy matter immediately.

### COPENHAGEN HOUSING: Prices Jump 20% YoY, Seller's Market Deepens
Copenhagen apartment prices hit ~62,900 DKK/m² median (~66,226 DKK/m² average), up 20% in one year. Rental vacancy is 1-2% citywide, near 0% in central areas. New construction permits face stricter environmental requirements, adding 8-12% to building costs and reducing new housing supply by ~15%. Forecasts suggest 3-3.5% further annual growth, with a possible 6-10% upside in well-connected neighborhoods.

**Life Impact: VERY HIGH** — If you're looking to buy in Copenhagen, the window is tightening. Supply constraints from green building regs are structural and won't ease soon. Renting is equally squeezed.

## 2026-04-24

### EU POLITICS: Orbán Is Out — Magyar Wins Hungarian Election in Landslide
The story from April 7 resolved dramatically. Péter Magyar's Tisza party won 138 of 199 seats (53.6%) on April 12, ending Orbán's 16-year rule. It's the largest vote share any Hungarian party has ever won in a free election — a two-thirds supermajority that gives Magyar the power to amend Hungary's constitution. Orbán conceded. The EU immediately approved a €90 billion loan to Ukraine and a 20th sanctions package against Russia — both previously blocked by Orbán. Magyar has signaled a pro-EU, anti-corruption reset. Chatham House analysts note that while Orbán is gone, "Orbánism" — nationalist populism, media capture, judicial erosion — may prove harder to dismantle.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — This reshapes EU decision-making overnight. Expect faster movement on EU defense, migration, and budget negotiations. The €90B Ukraine loan was unblocked within days. For Denmark and the EU broadly, the removal of Hungary's veto changes the political math on virtually every major dossier.

### EU POLITICS: Cyprus Summit Tackles Budget, Iran Fallout, and Energy Crisis
EU leaders met informally in Cyprus on April 23-24. Key topics: the 2028-2034 EU budget (proposed at €1.78 trillion, up €175B from the Commission's proposal), fallout from the Iran war on energy prices, and Europe's mutual defense posture after a drone attack on Cyprus. Zelenskyy addressed leaders. Parliament will vote next week on rule-of-law and fundamental rights assessments, and debate antisemitism following attacks in the Netherlands and Belgium. The EU is nearly out of budget headroom for combating the energy crisis, per the Financial Times.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — The next EU budget determines funding for everything from research grants to infrastructure. Energy prices driven by the Iran war are already hitting household costs. The defense debate could mean higher defense spending obligations for member states including Denmark.

### DANISH POLITICS: Coalition Talks Enter Second Month, Frederiksen Widens Net
Coalition negotiations have now dragged past the one-month mark. The Moderates' Lars Løkke Rasmussen said talks will take "at least another month." Frederiksen has broadened negotiations beyond the initial center-left focus — she's now meeting with the Liberals (Venstre) and Conservatives, after Venstre ruled out rejoining a government with the Social Democrats. A centrist arrangement with the Moderates remains the most likely outcome, but the expanding circle of parties signals difficulty in landing a deal. No government is expected before late May at the earliest.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — Denmark is effectively in political limbo. Major policy decisions on housing, defense spending, and Greenland strategy are frozen until a government forms. The longer this drags, the more it delays Denmark's response to the EU defense and budget negotiations happening right now.

Since last update: a ceasefire is technically in effect but fragile. Trump extended it at Pakistan's request to allow Iran to submit a unified negotiation proposal, but said an extension beyond that is "highly unlikely." **Life Impact: HIGH** — Energy prices remain elevated due to the blockade and Hormuz uncertainty. The EU summit specifically discussed the economic fallout. This war is directly affecting European energy costs and will likely drive the next EU budget cycle's priorities.

### AI: Anthropic Releases Claude Mythos Preview — A New Class of AI
Anthropic released Claude Mythos Preview on April 8, an entirely new model tier (not an Opus upgrade, codenamed Capybara). It's gated and restricted to defensive cybersecurity use cases. During testing, it found "thousands of high-severity vulnerabilities" in "every major operating system and web browser" and is the first model to solve a 32-step simulated corporate network attack end-to-end. Anthropic launched Project Glasswing — a consortium of 40+ organizations with monitored access to find and fix vulnerabilities in critical software. The UK's AISI published an evaluation of its cyber capabilities. Foreign Policy and BusinessToday both published analyses of the security implications. Separately, OpenAI released GPT-5.5, and the two are now compared head-to-head.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM-HIGH** — This is a step change in AI capability, specifically in cybersecurity. The gated access model shows Anthropic taking safety seriously, but the capabilities are real and will reshape both offensive and defensive security. If you work with software or infrastructure, this matters.

### 3D PRINTING: New York Proposes Print-Blocking Censorware — EFF Fighting Back
New York's 2026-2027 budget includes provisions requiring all 3D printers sold in the state to run print-blocking censorware that surveils every print job for forbidden designs (firearms). Possessing or sharing certain design files would become a felony. The EFF published a call to action in April, arguing the law is technically unworkable and a threat to the entire 3D printing ecosystem. Meanwhile at RAPID+TCT: Anycubic showed multicolor FDM and large-format resin printers, Bambu Lab retired its X1 series, Rice University demonstrated microwave-based 3D printing, and DyeMansion announced a new compact post-processing system.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM** — The NY bill is US-only but could set a precedent. If print-blocking censorware becomes normalized, it could affect printer firmware globally. The Bambu X1 retirement matters if you own one (support timeline unclear). Rice's microwave printing is early but interesting.
