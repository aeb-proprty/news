# Daily News Log

## 2026-04-23

### HUNGARY / EU POLITICS: Orbán Is Out — Magyar Wins Historic Supermajority
The April 12 election delivered a landslide. Péter Magyar's Tisza Party took 138 of 199 seats (53.6%) — the largest victory in a free Hungarian election ever. Orbán conceded. Magyar has a two-thirds supermajority, enough to amend Hungary's constitution. This removes the EU's most persistent veto blocker on Ukraine aid, migration policy, and rule-of-law enforcement. Expect a significant shift in EU decision-making speed.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — The EU's ability to act on defense, sanctions, and migration just got dramatically easier. This will ripple into Danish/EU policy discussions for years.

### DANISH POLITICS: Coalition Talks Pivot Right, Another Month Expected
Since the April 7 update: Løkke Rasmussen (Moderates) blocked any centre-left government dependent on the Red-Green Alliance on April 10, calling it "irresponsible and impossible." Frederiksen has pivoted to the Liberals and Conservatives. Løkke's preferred formula: Social Democrats + Social Liberals + Venstre + Conservatives + Moderates — a broad centrist five-party coalition. Bloomberg reports talks are expected to run at least another month. Denmark remains under a caretaker government as Greenland tensions with Trump add urgency.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — Still no government. The longer this drags, the longer housing, tax, and defense policy decisions are frozen. Løkke's centrist bloc formula would be historically unusual and could reshape Danish politics if it holds.

### MIDDLE EAST: Day 54 — Ceasefire in Name Only, Strait Still Closed
The April 8 ceasefire (brokered by Frederiksen among others) was extended indefinitely by Trump. But Iran seized two commercial ships in the Strait of Hormuz hours later. Trump's team said this didn't violate the ceasefire because "they weren't US or Israeli ships." The Strait has been effectively closed for ~2 months. The EU is now exploring emergency jet fuel imports from the US and new minimum reserve quotas. No actual flight cancellations yet, but the EU transport commissioner is under pressure. Oil markets remain volatile.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — Fuel prices, flight costs, and energy bills are all exposed. If you're flying this summer (Stockholm trips?), keep an eye on ticket prices. The EU jet fuel contingency plan signals real concern.

### AI: Anthropic Launches Claude Mythos Preview — Then It Gets Complicated
Anthropic launched Claude Mythos Preview on April 7 — a new tier above Opus, built for cybersecurity, autonomous coding, and long-running agents. The headline: it can find and exploit zero-day vulnerabilities in "every major operating system and web browser" with 99%+ of discovered vulnerabilities unpatched. Anthropic restricted it to a few dozen companies via "Project Glasswing," an industry consortium to fix foundational vulnerabilities. Then on April 22, reports emerged of unauthorized access to the restricted model — investigation ongoing.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM-HIGH** — This is a step change in AI capability. The cybersecurity implications are enormous. If you build anything on the internet, the security landscape just shifted. The unauthorized access story is worth watching.

### 3D PRINTING: Bambu Lab X2D Launches at $899, X1 Series Retired
Bambu Lab officially retired the entire X1 series (X1, X1 Carbon, X1E) on March 31. Spares guaranteed through 2031. The successor is the X2D — a dual-nozzle FDM printer with mechanical switching, designed to eliminate support removal pain. The X2D Combo enters at $899, which is $550 cheaper than the original X1 Kickstarter price. Not a toolchanger, but reviewers say the dual-nozzle setup is "incredibly effective." Tom's Hardware gave it strong marks.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM** — If you have an X1, it's still supported for 5 years. If you're considering an upgrade, the X2D is a compelling option at that price point. The dual-nozzle soluble support approach is a genuine QoL improvement.

### 3D PRINTING: New York Wants to Put Censorware on Every 3D Printer
New York's 2026-2027 budget includes provisions requiring all 3D printers sold in the state to run print-blocking software that scans every print for forbidden designs (targeting firearm parts). Possessing or sharing certain design files would be a Class E felony. The EFF is fighting it hard, calling it technically unfeasible and a threat to privacy, free expression, and innovation. The provision is buried in the state budget bill (S.9005 / A.10005).

**Life Impact: LOW-MEDIUM** — US-only and likely unconstitutional, but it sets a precedent that could inspire similar EU proposals. Worth watching if you care about maker freedom and open-source hardware.

## 2026-04-07

### DANISH POLITICS: Coalition Talks Drag On After Historic Election
The March 24 snap election left no bloc with a majority. Mette Frederiksen's Social Democrats won 38 seats but with their worst vote share (21.9%) since 1903. The Green Left surged to become the second-largest party (20 seats), and the Danish People's Party made significant gains. The Moderates are now kingmaker. Coalition negotiations are ongoing with no resolution yet. The Greenland/Trump crisis was the catalyst for the snap election and gave Frederiksen a rally-round-the-flag boost.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — A new government will directly affect tax policy, housing regulation, immigration rules, and Denmark's defense posture. If you're in Copenhagen, coalition outcomes on housing and green policy matter immediately.

### EU POLITICS: Parliament Simplifies AI Rules, Bans Nudifier AI
The European Parliament agreed on proposals to simplify the EU AI Act's application dates for high-risk systems and voted to ban AI "nudifier" systems outright. Separately, Parliament and Council reached agreement on a major EU Customs Code reform targeting e-commerce and product safety. New EU-wide anti-corruption criminal law framework was also adopted.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM-HIGH** — The AI rule simplification directly affects anyone working with or building AI systems in the EU. The customs reform will affect online shopping from non-EU sellers.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM** — An Orbán loss would reshape EU consensus-building, potentially accelerating decisions on defense, migration, and Ukraine support that affect all EU citizens.

### COPENHAGEN HOUSING: Prices Jump 20% YoY, Seller's Market Deepens
Copenhagen apartment prices hit ~62,900 DKK/m² median (~66,226 DKK/m² average), up 20% in one year. Rental vacancy is 1-2% citywide, near 0% in central areas. New construction permits face stricter environmental requirements, adding 8-12% to building costs and reducing new housing supply by ~15%. Forecasts suggest 3-3.5% further annual growth, with a possible 6-10% upside in well-connected neighborhoods.

**Life Impact: VERY HIGH** — If you're looking to buy in Copenhagen, the window is tightening. Supply constraints from green building regs are structural and won't ease soon. Renting is equally squeezed.
