### DANISH POLITICS: Frederiksen Re-appointed Formateur, Record-Long Coalition Talks Continue
UPDATE (previously covered Apr 7): Mette Frederiksen was re-appointed formateur on May 23 after meeting with King Frederik X, now backed by the Moderates and red-bloc parties. Coalition talks have shattered the historic record of 42 days and are entering their third month. Lars Lokke Rasmussen's Moderates remain the kingmaker, pushing for a cross-bloc centrist government (Social Democrats, Social Liberals, Venstre, Conservatives, and Moderates), while Frederiksen prefers a broader red-bloc coalition including the Green Left. Folkemodet on Bornholm (June 11-13) will be the next major political event. Denmark will NOT meet the EU Pay Transparency Directive's June 7 transposition deadline; Danish implementation is delayed to January 2027, with pay reporting obligations from September 2028.

**Life Impact: HIGH** -- The shape of this government will determine housing regulation, tax policy, green building requirements, and Denmark's defense posture for the coming years. The pay transparency delay means no immediate change for employers.

### EU POLITICS: Orban Defeated in Hungary; EU Foreign Investment Screening Tightens
UPDATE (previously covered Apr 7): Peter Magyar's Tisza party won a landslide victory on April 12, taking 141 of 199 seats (53.6%) with a two-thirds supermajority that allows constitutional amendments. Orban conceded on election night. Turnout hit a record 79.6%. This removes the single biggest EU veto blocker on Ukraine aid, defense spending, and migration policy. Meanwhile, the EU Parliament approved new foreign investment screening rules replacing measures expiring June 30 -- aimed at protecting critical sectors including steel. The Council is also advancing migration pact implementation, debating the future legal status of Ukrainian displaced persons, the Entry/Exit System and ETIAS rollout, and a regulation on European business digital wallets.

**Life Impact: HIGH** -- Magyar's supermajority fundamentally changes EU consensus dynamics. Expect faster movement on defense, migration, and Ukraine policy. The investment screening rules signal a more protectionist EU.

### MIDDLE EAST: US-Iran Tentative Deal Reached, Awaiting Trump Sign-off
UPDATE (previously covered Apr 7): The April 7-8 ceasefire was agreed but has been repeatedly violated. On May 25, the US conducted strikes in southern Iran targeting missile sites and boats in the Strait of Hormuz, described as "defensive." On May 28, US and Iranian negotiators reached a tentative deal to extend the ceasefire by 60 days and begin nuclear talks -- but Trump has not signed off and Iran has not confirmed. Key negotiation points: Strait of Hormuz navigation, Iran's 440.9 kg of 60%-enriched uranium, sanctions, reconstruction, and a long-term peace framework. Pakistan is mediating.

**Life Impact: HIGH** -- The Strait of Hormuz situation continues to affect global energy prices. A deal extension would stabilize oil markets; failure means renewed disruption. Watch fuel and energy costs.

### COPENHAGEN HOUSING: Nykredit Forecasts 13.7% Price Rise in 2026, Supply at Record Low
UPDATE (previously covered Apr 7): The market has tightened further. Nykredit now expects Copenhagen apartment prices to rise 13.7% in 2026 (up from the 3-3.5% baseline forecast in April), with 3.1% growth continuing in 2027. Supply of apartments in Copenhagen has hit the lowest level ever recorded. The city needs 5,000-7,000 new units annually but construction delivers only 3,000-4,000. Well-located properties sell in 14-21 days. Nordhavn is the most expensive area at ~98,000 DKK/m2. Entry-level central apartments start at 3.1-3.8M DKK. The growth neighborhoods to watch remain Sydhavn, Valby, and Orestad.

**Life Impact: VERY HIGH** -- If you're a property owner, your equity is growing fast. If you're looking to buy, the window is actively closing. As a board member of an ejerforening, rising values also affect insurance assessments and maintenance budgets.
