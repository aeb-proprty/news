## 2026-07-29

### IRAN-US WAR: Mutual Pause in Strikes Enters Day 3 — Peace Talks Uncertain
The US and Iran have both paused strikes for a third consecutive day as of July 27, following nearly two weeks of intense US bombardment of Iran in retaliation for attacks on ships in the Strait of Hormuz. Trump claims Iran requested the halt; Iran denies any negotiations are underway. The combat pause began Friday after UN envoy Waltz urged both sides to give diplomacy "some space." The core issue remains control of the Strait of Hormuz — Iran wants to collect transit fees, the US demands unrestricted passage. A compromise being negotiated would allow Iran to run vessel transit with fewer restrictions. Meanwhile, Trump denied reports of dwindling US munitions and posted AI-generated images of attacks. Iran separately summoned Ukrainian diplomats over a Caspian Sea attack. Gas prices in the US have surged throughout the conflict. The war began February 28 when the US and Israel struck Iran, killing Supreme Leader Khamenei.

**Life Impact: VERY HIGH** — Oil prices and energy costs remain elevated globally due to Strait of Hormuz disruptions. If the pause leads to a real ceasefire, expect fuel prices to ease. If it collapses, expect further spikes. Denmark's energy costs are directly affected.

### EU POLITICS: Google Hit with First-Ever DMA Fine — EUR 890M, Must Redesign Search in 60 Days
The European Commission fined Google EUR 890 million on July 23 for two breaches of the Digital Markets Act: EUR 460M for self-preferencing its own services (Flights, Hotels) in search results, and EUR 430M for preventing app developers from steering consumers to purchases outside the Play Store. Google has 60 days (~September 21) to comply or face penalties of up to 5% of Alphabet's daily global revenue. This is the first fine under the DMA and sets the enforcement precedent for all designated gatekeepers.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM-HIGH** — If Google is forced to redesign search results, it could change how you find services online in the EU. The Play Store ruling may lower app prices as developers can direct you to cheaper alternatives.

### AI: OpenAI Launches GPT-5.6 — Three-Tier Model Family (Sol, Terra, Luna)
OpenAI released GPT-5.6 publicly on July 9 after government review. Instead of one model, it ships three tiers: Sol (flagship, hard problems, $5/$30 per M tokens), Terra (everyday use, matches old GPT-5.5, $2.50/$15), and Luna (fast/cheap bulk work, $1/$6). Sol powers ChatGPT's reasoning modes on paid plans. New features include "max reasoning" settings and an "ultra" mode running parallel sub-agents. This was previewed June 26 for ~20 government-approved organizations before the US Commerce Department cleared broader access.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM-HIGH** — If you use AI tools professionally, this is a significant capability and pricing shift. The three-tier approach means you can match cost to task complexity. The "ultra" parallel agent mode is a step toward true autonomous AI work.

### AI/MARKETS: Big Tech AI Capex Hits $725B — Wall Street Panics
The four hyperscalers (Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft, Meta) are on track to spend ~$725 billion combined on AI infrastructure in 2026, up 77% from ~$410B in 2025. Alphabet triggered a sell-off on July 22 when it raised guidance to $195-205B, causing its stock to drop 6% and dragging Amazon (-4%), Meta (-3%), and Microsoft (-2%) down. Morgan Stanley expects combined capex to exceed $1 trillion in 2027. The spending is almost entirely on GPU clusters, custom chips, and data centers.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM** — This spending is what keeps AI services available, fast, and improving. But the market reaction signals investor anxiety about whether the AI revenue payoff will match the investment. If it doesn't, expect consolidation and potentially higher AI service prices.

### COPENHAGEN HOUSING: Growth Moderates to 10% H1 — Buyers Pushing to Suburbs
Copenhagen apartment prices rose 10.1% in the first half of 2026, moderating from the 20%+ surge in 2025. Average price per m2 is now around DKK 75,000. Activity has "slowed somewhat" in central Copenhagen while Aarhus and commuter-belt areas are moving at full speed — a clear signal that buyers are being priced out to suburbs. Nykredit forecasts 13.7% full-year growth; Realkredit Danmark is more cautious, arguing prices are reaching demand limits. Danmarks Nationalbank has warned that demand is increasingly driven by expectations of further appreciation rather than fundamentals.

**Life Impact: VERY HIGH** — The Nationalbank warning is a yellow flag. When appreciation expectations drive demand, you're in bubble territory. The shift to suburbs means commuter-distance neighborhoods (Valby, Hvidovre, Brondby) may be the last affordable window. If you're looking to buy, the moderation from 20% to 10% growth is welcome but prices at DKK 75K/m2 remain historically extreme.
