### DANISH POLITICS: Frederiksen III Government Formed — Greenest Coalition in Danish History
After 10 weeks of negotiations following the March snap election, Mette Frederiksen was formally appointed PM for a third term on June 3. The new minority coalition consists of Social Democrats, Socialist People's Party (SF), Radikale Venstre, and the Moderates — holding 82 of 179 seats, with confidence-and-supply from Enhedslisten. The coalition's founding document leads with a hard green commitment: clean drinking water, sustainable food production, and affordable green energy. Security and NATO commitments are reinforced, with increased military spending. Alternativet (your party) secured 5 seats and sits outside the coalition but within the broader left bloc. The Greenland/Trump crisis remains unresolved — Frederiksen continues to firmly reject any sovereignty transfer, calling it "the end of NATO" if it happened. Trump backed off force/tariff threats at Davos in January but the diplomatic tension persists.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — The government is now in place and governing. Its green-transition-first agenda will directly shape housing regulation, energy costs, and building requirements in Copenhagen. Alternativet's 5 seats give the party influence as a potential support party on green and social issues.

### MIDDLE EAST: Iran-US Ceasefire Signed But Already Fracturing — Talks Resume in Qatar
Massive developments since April. The US-Iran war escalated through April-May, with the assassination of Supreme Leader Khamenei in airstrikes. A 60-day ceasefire MOU was signed June 17, including reopening the Strait of Hormuz to commercial shipping and sanctions relief discussions. But the ceasefire is already cracking — Iran attacked a cargo ship near Oman last week, triggering US retaliatory strikes and Iranian counterstrikes on US bases in Kuwait and Bahrain. Both sides have now paused strikes again and agreed to meet in Doha this week (Tuesday). Qatar and Pakistan are mediating. The core dispute: competing interpretations of the MOU's terms on the Strait of Hormuz. Thousands dead across Iran, Lebanon, Israel, and Gulf states. Millions displaced.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — Global fuel prices remain volatile. The Strait of Hormuz situation directly affects European energy costs. If talks collapse again, expect another oil price spike. The fragile ceasefire is the single biggest wildcard for the global economy right now.

### EU: New €3 Customs Duty on Low-Value Parcels Starts TODAY
Effective July 1, 2026, all goods entering the EU valued under €150 are subject to a new flat €3 customs duty per item type (by tariff heading). Buy 5 t-shirts = €3. Buy 3 t-shirts + a watch = €6 (two tariff categories). This kills the old duty-free exemption for cheap e-commerce imports, mainly targeting Temu/Shein-style shopping. It's a transitional measure until the EU Customs Data Hub goes online July 2028, when normal product-specific duties will apply. Also today: Ireland takes over the EU Council presidency. The European Parliament plenary July 6-9 will debate Ukraine and Moldova's EU membership progress, air passenger rights, and social security coordination.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM-HIGH** — If you order anything from non-EU e-commerce (AliExpress, Temu, Shein, etc.), every order now costs a few euros more. The structural goal is to level the playing field for EU-based retailers. The Ukraine/Moldova membership votes could reshape EU budgets and agricultural policy.

### AI: Anthropic's Fable 5 Returns Today After US Export Control Drama
On June 9, Anthropic launched Claude Fable 5 — then on June 12, the US Commerce Department slapped export controls on it, barring access for ALL foreign nationals, including non-citizen Anthropic employees. The trigger: Amazon researchers found a jailbreak bypassing safety guardrails. Anthropic had no choice but to disable Fable 5 and its sibling Mythos 5 globally. After 2.5 weeks and a new safety filter (blocking the technique in 99%+ of attempts), the export controls were lifted June 30. Fable 5 returns today across Claude.ai, the Claude Platform, Claude Code, and Claude Cowork. This is the first time the US used export controls to pull an AI model post-launch — a precedent with implications for the entire industry. Separately, Trump signed an AI executive order focused on cybersecurity and critical infrastructure, and the Stanford 2026 AI Index shows organizational AI adoption at 88%.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — As a developer working with AI tools, the export control precedent matters enormously. A government can now yank access to an AI model overnight based on a security finding. If you use Claude products (which you do), Fable 5 is back today. The incident also signals that AI safety/jailbreak research has direct regulatory consequences.
