# Daily News Log

## 2026-04-14

### EU POLITICS: Orbán OUT — Péter Magyar Wins Hungary in Landslide
Hungary's election (April 12) is decided: Magyar's Tisza Party won 138 of 199 seats on 53.6% of the vote. Orbán's Fidesz collapsed to 55 seats (37.8%). Turnout was a record 77%+. The two-thirds majority lets Magyar amend the constitution. Orbán has conceded after 16 years in power. Magyar is calling for the president to resign and has laid out a reform-heavy vision (anti-corruption, judicial reset, re-engagement with Brussels).

**Life Impact: MEDIUM-HIGH** — This changes EU politics immediately. Expect the EU-Ukraine aid log-jam to break within weeks, faster sanctions decisions, and a realignment of the EU's "illiberal bloc." For Denmark specifically, it reduces Frederiksen's friction on EU-level files.

### DANISH POLITICS: Frederiksen Pivots Right After Løkke Blocks Red-Bloc Path
Coalition talks are in week four. On April 10, Lars Løkke Rasmussen (Moderates) formally blocked any centre-left government dependent on the Red-Green Alliance. On April 13, Frederiksen began substantive talks with right-leaning parties. Sticking points: her proposed wealth tax on high-income individuals (right-bloc opposes) and pesticide restrictions (farming lobby and centre-right oppose). No new PM named yet; Frederiksen remains caretaker.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — A right-leaning compromise government is now more likely than the red-bloc scenario from two weeks ago. That shifts the tax, housing, and environmental policy outlook meaningfully. The wealth tax being on the chopping block is directly relevant if you own property in Copenhagen.

### MIDDLE EAST: Ceasefire Came and Went — Trump Orders US Navy Blockade of Hormuz
A lot has happened. April 8: US-Iran agreed a two-week ceasefire mediated by Pakistan; Iran promised to fully reopen the Strait of Hormuz. April 12: JD Vance announced the Islamabad talks collapsed — Iran rejected US "red lines" (end enrichment, dismantle facilities, surrender HEU, stop funding proxies, stop charging Hormuz tolls). April 13: Trump ordered the US Navy to blockade and demine the Strait starting today, and said US ships will intercept vessels that paid Iran's tolls. Brent spiked above $103, now trading ~$97.45 (down ~12% on the week as markets price in US control of the strait). Iran had been charging $1M+ per ship transit.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — Naval blockade is a step-change in escalation risk. Expect volatility at the pump and in heating costs through spring. If blockade goes smoothly, relief rally; if Iran mines or fires on US assets, oil goes back through $110. Watch your gas bill.

### UKRAINE: Peace-Deal Signals Move Markets
Ukraine's military intel chief Kyrylo Budanov signaled that a resolution with Russia "may not take long." Defense stocks slumped hard (Rheinmetall, Hensoldt, Leonardo all down 7%+; Goldman's defense basket -5.1%). Construction stocks rallied on rebuild bets. Separately, the European Commission formally proposed a €45B Ukraine support package for 2026 (€16.7B budget support + €28.3B defense-industry capacity).

**Life Impact: MEDIUM** — If a ceasefire lands, European energy and food prices ease, reconstruction contracts become a growth story, and defense budget pressure moderates. If it falls through, the €45B package becomes the story.

### COPENHAGEN HOUSING: Nykredit Forecasts +17.4% for 2026 — Much Higher Than Nordea's 4.9%
Nykredit's April 2026 forecast projects Copenhagen apartment prices rising **17.4% in 2026** — a striking number. Nordea is much more conservative at +4.9% for 2026 and +4.2% for 2027. Realkredit Danmark sees +6-6.5% nationally. The divergence reflects genuine disagreement about whether 2025's 24% Copenhagen surge was a spike or the new baseline. Supply is at record lows — listings are the lowest ever measured and average time-on-market hasn't been this short since the mid-2000s bubble. A 2-bedroom in the brokvartere is now routinely over 3M DKK.

**Life Impact: VERY HIGH** — The 13-point forecast spread between Nykredit and Nordea matters. If Nykredit is right, waiting costs you serious money; if Nordea is right, panic-buying at current prices is the risk. For your Fioniahus2 context (Havneholmen area), waterfront Sydhavn is in the fast-appreciation band (8-15% YoY). Worth raising at tonight's generalforsamling whether the association is tracking how these forecasts affect ejerlejlighed valuations.

### 3D PRINTING: Bambu Lab X2D Launches TODAY (16:00 CEST); Prusa + Bondtech Announce INDX Tool Changer
Bambu Lab's new flagship — widely expected to be the **X2D** dual-extrusion printer — reveals today at 16:00 CEST under the tagline "Xcellence made simple." Positioned between the P2S and H-series: engineering-grade, heated chamber for ABS/ASA/nylon composites, dedicated support material. Separately, Josef Prusa announced a Bondtech partnership for the **INDX**, a 4-to-8-head tool changer that mounts on the CORE One — €499 (4 tools) / €699 (8 tools), sold by Bondtech. Both launches arrive in the same week as RAPID+TCT (Boston).

**Life Impact: MEDIUM** — If you've been waiting to upgrade, today is the day to watch the stream. Heated-chamber engineering materials in a desktop form are a real capability unlock. The Prusa INDX at sub-€500 is aggressive pricing — it undercuts AMS-style multi-material approaches for people who want true tool changes without filament waste.

### DENMARK ECONOMY: 2026 Growth Forecast Nudged Up to 2.2%
The government raised its 2026 Danish growth forecast from 2.1% to 2.2%. Inflation projected to fall from 1.9% to 1.1% in 2026 (helped by cuts to indirect and electricity taxes), then back toward 2% in 2027. Budget surplus 1.1% of GDP in 2026. One caveat: pharma growth revised DOWN as obesity-drug competition (Novo's GLP-1 franchise) bites — layoffs already announced.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM** — Lower inflation + electricity tax cut = modest real-income gain. Pharma/Novo weakness matters for Danish equities and the krone; also watch for knock-on effects on Copenhagen's high-end housing, where pharma salaries are a big demand driver.

The March 24 snap election left no bloc with a majority. Mette Frederiksen's Social Democrats won 38 seats but with their worst vote share (21.9%) since 1903. The Green Left surged to become the second-largest party (20 seats), and the Danish People's Party made significant gains. The Moderates are now kingmaker. Coalition negotiations are ongoing with no resolution yet. The Greenland/Trump crisis was the catalyst for the snap election and gave Frederiksen a rally-round-the-flag boost.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — A new government will directly affect tax policy, housing regulation, immigration rules, and Denmark's defense posture. If you're in Copenhagen, coalition outcomes on housing and green policy matter immediately.
