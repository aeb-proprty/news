# Daily News Log

## 2026-05-01

### IRAN WAR: War Powers Deadline Hits Today — Senate Fails to Act

Today marks the 60-day War Powers Resolution deadline for the Iran war. Yesterday the Senate rejected a resolution to force withdrawal, 47-50. Sen. Susan Collins (R-ME) broke ranks for the first time, calling the deadline "a requirement, not a suggestion." Sen. Fetterman (D-PA) was again the lone Democrat voting against. The Trump administration argues the April 7-8 ceasefire "pauses the clock," but the conflict has shifted to a Strait of Hormuz standoff. The last Gulf oil/LNG tankers have now reached Europe — no more are coming until the strait reopens. Democrats are exploring suing Trump over an "illegal" war. The war's estimated cost has hit $25 billion. Brent crude surged to $80-82/barrel and analysts warn $100 is possible. Europe faces diesel shortages and LNG competition with Asia (12-14% of EU LNG comes from Qatar via the strait).

**Life Impact: VERY HIGH** — Energy prices in Denmark/EU are about to climb further as Gulf supply dries up. Watch your electricity and heating bills. The constitutional crisis in the US also adds geopolitical uncertainty. This is the single biggest macro risk to your cost of living right now.

### DANISH POLITICS: Coalition Talks Enter 5th Week — Frederiksen Pivots Right

Frederiksen's coalition negotiations have entered their fifth week with a notable shift: she is now engaging right-leaning parties after initial talks with the Green Left and Social Liberals stalled. Venstre has formally ruled out governing with the Social Democrats. Lars Løkke Rasmussen's Moderates (14 seats) remain kingmaker but insist on a cross-bloc solution — neither pure red nor pure blue. A deal is expected sometime in May but the shape of the government remains genuinely uncertain.

Separately, **today (May 1) a new deportation law takes effect**, making it easier to deport foreign nationals sentenced to 1+ year for serious crimes, including ankle-monitor provisions. Denmark's electricity tax has also been reduced to the EU minimum for 2026-2027, which should provide some buffer against the energy price increases from the Iran crisis.

**Life Impact: HIGH** — The government composition will determine tax policy (wealth tax proposal still on the table), housing regulation, and green building rules. The electricity tax cut is immediate relief. If you're on the condo board, the lower electricity tax helps common-area costs.

### COPENHAGEN HOUSING: Prices Accelerate — Average Listing Now 73,826 DKK/m²

Average listing prices for Copenhagen apartments have climbed to 73,826 DKK/m² in April, up from the ~66,226 DKK/m² sold-price average reported in early April. Ejerlejligheder in Copenhagen rose 24% through 2025. Nordea and Nykredit now forecast 4.9% growth in 2026 and 4.2% in 2027 — higher than the 3-3.5% forecast from a month ago. Supply remains structurally constrained; no significant new housing construction is expected in the capital region near-term.

**Life Impact: VERY HIGH** — The market is accelerating, not stabilizing. If you're considering buying, every quarter of delay costs roughly 1.2% in price. If you own, your equity is growing but so are your neighbors' expectations at board meetings for building improvements.

### AI: Novo Nordisk Partners with OpenAI — Denmark's Biggest AI Deal

Denmark's largest company announced a strategic partnership with OpenAI on April 14 to integrate AI across its entire operation — from drug discovery and clinical trials to manufacturing, supply chains, and commercial operations. Pilot programs are launching now with full integration targeted by end of 2026. The deal aims to accelerate identification of new obesity and diabetes treatments. It's structured with strict data governance and human oversight.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM-HIGH** — This is Denmark's biggest company going all-in on AI. It signals massive demand for AI talent in Copenhagen/Denmark and could accelerate drug development for conditions affecting millions. If you work in tech/AI in Denmark, the talent market just got hotter.

### AI: Cambridge Brain-Chip Cuts AI Energy 70%

University of Cambridge researchers published a breakthrough memristor using modified hafnium oxide that mimics how neurons store and process information. Switching currents are a million times lower than conventional devices, with hundreds of stable conductance levels for analog computing. Limitation: fabrication currently requires 700°C, above standard semiconductor tolerances. Published in Science Advances.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM** — If this scales (and the temperature problem is solved), it could dramatically cut the cost and energy footprint of AI infrastructure. Relevant to the energy discussion above — AI's power hunger is a real concern.

### AI: Humans Still Beat AI Agents on Complex Tasks (Nature)

A Nature study found that human scientists significantly outperform the best AI agents on complex, multi-step research tasks. While AI agents excel at narrow, well-defined problems, they struggle with the kind of open-ended reasoning and creative problem-solving that characterizes real scientific work. Also notable: the US Air Force successfully deployed "WarMatrix," an AI wargaming system running simulations 10,000x faster than real time, with 150+ participants.

**Life Impact: LOW-MEDIUM** — Reassuring if you worry about AI replacing knowledge workers wholesale. The military AI application is more concerning from a geopolitics perspective.

### EU: Meta Found in Breach of Digital Services Act Over Minors

The European Commission has preliminarily found Meta's Instagram and Facebook in breach of the DSA for failing to protect minors under 13 from accessing their platforms. Separately, the EU's Entry/Exit System (EES) is now fully operational across all Schengen countries since April 10 — replacing passport stamps with biometric registration for non-EU nationals. Parliament is also voting on the 2028-2034 EU budget this week, and the Commission proposed emergency energy relief measures for households in light of the Iran crisis.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM** — The Meta ruling could lead to significant changes in how social platforms work in the EU. The EES matters if you travel with non-EU friends/family or have visitors — expect longer border waits. The energy relief proposals could directly lower bills.

### 3D PRINTING: Support-Free Titanium, New SLA, EU Dental Approval

South Korean INNOSPACE claims the first commercialized support-free metal AM process for titanium components (aerospace/defense). 3D Systems launched the SLA 825 Dual with 22% larger build volume and 25% faster speeds. Their NextDent dental printing solution received EU Class IIa medical device certification and ships May 4. Elegoo released the CANVAS firmware upgrade for the Centauri Carbon printer. A research team developed conductive 3D-printable resin using PEDOT:PSS and nano-graphite.

**Life Impact: LOW-MEDIUM** — Support-free titanium printing is a big deal for the industry but not consumer-facing yet. The conductive resin is interesting for electronics hobbyists. If you have an Elegoo Centauri, the CANVAS update is worth grabbing.

### CROSSFIT: Semifinals Underway — 12 Events, 9 Weekends, Global Expansion

The 2026 Semifinals kicked off April 17 and run through June 15 — nine consecutive weekends. CrossFit expanded to 12 Semifinal events globally, including South Korea, South Africa, Brazil, and Spain. The Legends Championship Del Mar Semifinal has completed. New this year: Masters and Teams can qualify for the Games at in-person Semifinals. The Adaptive division is now run by WheelWOD. Games still set for July 24-26 at SAP Center, San Jose.

**Life Impact: LOW** — Relevant if you follow the competitive season. The global expansion is good for the community. Keep an eye on European Semifinal results if you want to track who's heading to the Games.

### INNOVATION: Sodium-Ion Batteries and Gene Editing Go Commercial

Sodium-ion batteries (using abundant salt-based materials) are reaching commercial viability as a cheaper, safer lithium alternative for grid storage and affordable EVs. First hybrid (perovskite-silicon) solar cells are expected to hit the market this year with mass-production-ready efficiency. In biotech, personalized gene editing is becoming reality — "Baby KJ" received the first bespoke gene-editing treatment, with clinical trials now planned and potential FDA approval within years.

**Life Impact: MEDIUM** — Sodium-ion batteries could significantly reduce EV and energy storage costs within 2-3 years. Gene editing is longer-term but transformative for rare diseases.
